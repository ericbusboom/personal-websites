Of the many [versions of the famous quote](<http://quoteinvestigator.com/2010/11/08/writing-about-music/>) that "writing about music is like dancing about architecture" is that "writing about music is as illogical as singing about economics." Both of these activities are actually quite common. [Dancing about architecture](<http://youtu.be/UI9w73S36As>) is a sport of irony, and not only are there songs about economics, the appropriately named [Merle Hazard has dedicated a career to it](<http://www.merlehazard.com/>). There is even a [stand up economist](<http://standupeconomist.com/>), who is quite funny, if you can understand the jokes. I propose that the next version of the quote involve something that actually is ridiculous and should not be done, inappropriate visualizations, of which I think I have found an excellent example: a heatmap of benchmarks. Benchmarks are the metal medallions that surveyors place in sidewalks and mountain tops to provide a repeatable location for surveying. They are stamped with numbers and named, and if you are surveying, it is important to know where they are so you can easily find them. ![](/uploads/2013/02/benchmark.jpg) It is natural, then, to want [a map of their locations](<http://www.cityofchicago.org/content/dam/city/depts/water/supp_info/Benchmarks/BMMap.pdf>): [![Portion of benchmark map for Chicago. ](/uploads/12/2013/05/Chicago-Benchmarks.png)](</uploads/12/2013/05/Chicago-Benchmarks.png>) Portion of benchmark map for Chicago.   While you might want to know where the benchmarks are located, it is highly unlikely that you would every want to know the areal density of benchmarks. There is probably no one who has asked what part of the city has the most benchmarks per square meter. No one cares what is the probability of finding a benchmark when they look down. And yet, the Chicago data repository [has a heatmap of benchmarks](<https://data.cityofchicago.org/Buildings/Elevation-Heat-Map/vfd5-f3kt>): [![Heatmap of Chicago benchmarks. ](/uploads/12/2013/05/Benchmark-Heatmap.png)](</uploads/12/2013/05/Benchmark-Heatmap.png>) Heatmap of Chicago benchmarks.   This map is generated by Socarata, the software that Chicago uses for it's data repository, so it was probably very easy to create. It's possible that a user happened to look as the base datafile, clicked on a button to create the heatmap, and the software automatically made it available to the public. Regardless, this presentation, and others like it, is useless, and it clutters up the repository, obscuring the data that people actually want. This is why our organization has "Library" in the name: I want to emphasize that there should be a person, a librarian, who talks to users and then makes decisions about how to present the data collection. It's the difference between a well ordered research library and a pile of books in a warehouse. We're not there yet ( our repository is a bit of a mess, sorry ) but that is the goal.
