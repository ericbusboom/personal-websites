## Status: Planned

The building envelope — the physical separator between the conditioned interior and the exterior — is one of the most complex coordination points in this project. It must integrate the solar roof, structural steel, existing stucco walls, and interior finishes into a continuous weather, thermal, and fire barrier.

### Envelope components

The current concept includes:

- **Roof**: Standing-seam metal over self-adhered underlayment on metal deck (713 sq ft main + 131 sq ft hip cap)
- **Wall assembly** (inside existing stucco): Metal stud framing, R-19 batt insulation, 6-mil vapor barrier, 5/8″ Type X drywall (fire code), interior metal panels
- **Interior metal panels**: 2″ insulated metal panels, 665 sq ft net
- **Openings**: North loading door, west door, 3 north windows, balcony sliding door

### Coordination challenges

1. **Thermal bridging**: The steel frame penetrates the envelope at every column. Each penetration needs thermal break detailing.
2. **Moisture**: Existing stucco walls must be evaluated for water intrusion before enclosing them behind new insulation and panels
3. **Fire separation**: The Type X drywall requirement comes from fire code; the exact fire separation distance to property lines determines the assembly rating
4. **Ventilation**: An enclosed space above a garage needs mechanical or natural ventilation per code
5. **Solar roof integration**: The standing-seam metal roof must accommodate PV panel mounting without compromising the waterproofing

### Design decisions needed

- Roof drainage plan (gutters and downspouts, or alternative)
- Condensation management strategy
- Vapor barrier placement (climate zone determination)
- Flashing details at all steel column penetrations
- Hip cap transition detailing
- Exterior-grade door and window specifications
